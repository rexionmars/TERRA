\section*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Preface}

This document describes how TERRA is operated: the screens in the order they
appear, what each field does, what a run returns, and what the returned numbers
mean.

\campo{Who it is for} Whoever is going to operate the application. It assumes
no familiarity with the code or with remote sensing, but it does assume the
reader knows what they want to measure. Domain terms such as the
Jeffries-Matusita distance are used where the screen uses them, and defined at
the point they first appear.

\campo{What it does not cover} The derivation of the methods. Where a
formulation is cited, it comes with the reference and without the development.
Installation is also out of scope, with one exception: the Python interpreter,
covered in section~2, because it is the precondition for every task and the
place a first run usually stops.

\campo{How it is organised} Sections 2 to 7 follow the order of use: the
environment, the main screen, classification, the other products, the Studio
and the export. Each task opens with a summary block naming the panel, the
preconditions and the output, then continues in numbered steps.

The field tables, the editor catalogue and the message list are in the
appendices. They are reference: the reader returns to them with a specific
question, and in the middle of a procedure they interrupt what they were there
to support. Short tables that carry the argument of the paragraph beside them
stayed where they were.

\campo{Three conventions} Interface labels appear in \texttt{monospace},
exactly as they are on screen. Messages the application displays are quoted
verbatim, in \msg{italic between quotation marks}. A caveat marked with a rule
on the left bounds what has just been stated, and is not ornament: each one
corresponds to a limitation the application itself declares on screen.

\begin{aviso}
The figures are screenshots of the application in use, with real data. The
values in them belong to specific runs over specific areas and serve to locate
each element on the screen. They are not reference values.
\end{aviso}

\campo{Version and validation} This document describes the application as it
stood when the document was written. Defaults, ranges and messages were read
from the code, and change with it.

\section{What this guide covers}

TERRA classifies land cover from Sentinel-2 time series. Over the same area and
the same period it also produces band compositions, surface water mapping, and
a flood envelope derived from the terrain. The application is desktop: it runs
on the user's machine, with no server.

Appendix~\ref{ap:messages} collects the refusal messages and the conditions
reported during a run, for every task.

\section{Before you start: the interpreter}

The processing is carried out by a separate Python process, not by the Go
binary. The application opens without that process, but every analysis task
fails while it is unavailable. This is the precondition for all the sections
that follow.

% display it can go back to \linewidth like the rest.
\terrafig[.46\linewidth]{png-images/impressao/boot.png}
  {The opening screen. The application locates the data directory, the
   \emph{sidecar} and the model, and checks the interpreter before showing the
   map. Missing components are recorded in this list.}

\campo{How to tell it is missing} On start-up, when the sidecar does not
respond, the application shows: \msg{TERRA started, but the analysis sidecar
did not respond. Settings > System reports what is wrong.} On invoking an
analysis without a valid environment it shows \msg{Analyses cannot run yet ---
opening Settings > System} and navigates to that page.

\campo{Procedure} In \texttt{Settings} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{System}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=.25em]
  \item Read the \texttt{In use} block. It names the current interpreter and
        how it was chosen (table~\ref{tab:origin}).
  \item If the notice \msg{No analysis can run yet} is present, the line after
        it gives the cause: the interpreter could not be started, its version
        is below the minimum, or packages the sidecar imports are missing.
  \item Under \texttt{Interpreters on this machine}, choose a candidate.
        \texttt{Use as is} adopts the interpreter as it stands.
        \texttt{Build environment} builds an environment from it and installs
        the dependencies.
  \item For the neural models, install \texttt{torch} under \texttt{Optional
        models}. Without it the Temporal Transformer and Prithvi-EO 2.0 are
        unavailable; everything else works.
\end{enumerate}

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/settings.png}
  {\texttt{Settings} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{System}. \texttt{In use} identifies
   the current interpreter and says whether it is ready. \texttt{Interpreters
   on this machine} lists the candidates. \texttt{Optional models} lists the
   large downloads that do not come with the default install.
   \texttt{Locations} gives the origin of each component.}
  {fig:settings}

\begin{aviso}
The environment TERRA builds sits beside the database, not inside the
application, so updating the application does not remove it. An interpreter
adopted with \texttt{Use as is} is the machine's own, with whatever packages it
already carries. Packages installed only in the user directory may be available
to the terminal and not to the application.

The \texttt{GEOSENSE\_} prefix has been replaced by \texttt{TERRA\_}. A variable
with the old prefix stops being read without producing a symptom; the page
lists any it finds.
\end{aviso}

\section{The main screen}

The operating screens for classification, water and flood share the same
structure. What varies is the content of the left-hand panel and of the
result column.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/central-task-hub.png}
  {An operating screen, with an area drawn and no result yet. The numbers
   correspond to the items listed below.}
  {fig:anatomy}

\campo{1 --- Map controls} Zoom in and out, choice of basemap provider, drawing
the area, and access to the overlay configuration.

\campo{1.1 --- \texttt{Overlay Tools}} Renames the area, changes the polygon
colour, controls the opacity of each overlay (0.75 by default) and shows the
gallery of overlays generated for that area. While no run exists, the gallery
is empty.

\campo{2 --- The area of interest} The polygon drawn on the map. It can be
drawn by hand, imported from a file, or obtained by searching for a place. It
defines the extent of every subsequent operation.

\campo{3 --- The time window} The bar at the foot of the screen. The axis is
the same in every mode; what it controls varies. In classification it is the
scene acquisition period.

\campo{4 --- The action panel} Configures the run. The fields vary with the
operating mode. The column ends in the run button.

\campo{5 --- The operating modes} The navigation column. Modes with
sub-contexts show them below: \texttt{Map} contains \texttt{Classification},
\texttt{Compositions} and \texttt{Surface water}.

\campo{6 --- The header} On the left, the project selector, under which saved
runs and studios are filed. On the right, the coordinates of the map centre,
the zoom level, the basemap credit with its date, the button that opens the
Studio, and the one that switches the layout between side column and bar over
the map.

\section{Classifying land cover}

\begin{ficha}
  \item[Panel] \texttt{Map} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Classification}, headed
        \texttt{New classification}
  \item[Preconditions] A valid Python environment, an area, and start and end
        dates. The \texttt{Classify} button stays disabled while any of them is
        missing.
  \item[Output] A class layer over the area, saved to the active project
\end{ficha}

\campo{Procedure}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=.3em]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{01 Area}}. Draw a polygon on the map, search for a
        place, or use \texttt{Import} to load a file. The block indicator
        changes from \msg{No area defined} to \msg{Area defined}.
        \texttt{Clear} removes the current area.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{02 Period \& scenes}}. Set the dates, the maximum cloud
        cover and the monthly-best option (table~\ref{tab:classify}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{03 Model}}. Choose the model. With Prithvi-EO 2.0 a
        further choice appears between \texttt{Pixel} and \texttt{Patch}.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{04 Mode}}. \texttt{Map} uses the whole temporal stack.
        \texttt{Temporal} accumulates retention across the period and is
        disabled outside Random Forest, with the reason given as
        \msg{Temporal retention runs on Random Forest.}
  \item Press \texttt{Classify}. The progress bar replaces the button label
        with \msg{Classifying} while the run proceeds.
  \item On completion the application shows \msg{Classification complete ---
        N scenes (saved)} with a \texttt{View analysis} action.
\end{enumerate}

\campo{What comes back} A class layer over the area and a result panel giving
the number of scenes used, the interval actually covered, and the share of each
class.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/central-task-result-tocantins-AOI.png}
  {A completed classification. The \texttt{Result} panel names the scenes used
   and the interval covered, narrower than the one requested because cloudy
   scenes are dropped by the filter, and lists the share of each class.
   \texttt{Analysis} opens the full reading; \texttt{+ New} starts another run
   over the same area.}
  {fig:result}

\begin{objecao}
The \texttt{conf} value shown beside the scenes is the mean vote fraction of
the winning class, not the probability of being right. It is not a calibrated
measure, and under domain shift, meaning training in one region and applying in
another, it can move in the opposite direction to accuracy. \texttt{conf 67\%}
does not correspond to 67\% accuracy. The confidence layer legend, under
\texttt{Analysis}, repeats this caveat.
\end{objecao}

\campo{Reference cover} The \texttt{Analyze land cover} button, above
\texttt{Classify}, fetches MapBiomas cover for the same area. It requires an
area; without one the button reports \msg{Draw or select an area first}.

\campo{Where runs are kept} Every saved run goes to the \texttt{Project hub},
filed under the active project.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/project-hub.png}
  {The \texttt{Project hub}. Each stored run can be reopened over the map or
   loaded into the Studio. Runs are also deleted from this screen.}
  {fig:hub}

\section{The other products}

All of them start from the same area and the same time axis as
figure~\ref{fig:anatomy}, and each replaces the action panel with its own.

\subsection{Band compositions}

\begin{ficha}
  \item[Panel] \texttt{Map} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Compositions}
  \item[Preconditions] An area, a period, and one scene selected from the list
  \item[Output] An RGB layer over the area
\end{ficha}

Three bands are combined into the red, green and blue channels. No model is
involved: the values are measured reflectance. The band combination makes
visible features that a true-colour composition does not distinguish.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=.25em]
  \item Set the area and the period, and list the available scenes. Without a
        period the application answers \msg{Set the acquisition period.};
        without an area, \msg{Define an area: draw, search, or load an
        example.}
  \item Select a scene. With none selected the request answers \msg{Select a
        scene first.}
  \item Choose the composition. The presets are \texttt{True color},
        \texttt{False color IR}, \texttt{Agriculture} and \texttt{SWIR}; the
        available indices are \texttt{NDVI}, \texttt{NDWI}, \texttt{NDMI} and
        \texttt{EVI}.
\end{enumerate}

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/map-composition.png}
  {\texttt{Map} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Compositions}. The layer legend states
   which band was assigned to which channel.}
  {fig:composition}

\subsection{Surface water}

\begin{ficha}
  \item[Panel] \texttt{Map} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Surface water}
  \item[Preconditions] An area and a period
  \item[Output] A raster giving the proportion of dates on which each pixel was
        classified as water, on a fixed 0 to 1 scale
\end{ficha}

The panel has four blocks: \texttt{01 Area}, \texttt{02 Period},
\texttt{03 Index} and \texttt{04 Run}. The index is computed on every date in
the period, with no trained model.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering

\label{tab:water}

The cut is at zero, the literature threshold for all three indices. An Otsu

The result separates permanent water from water present on some of the dates.
Two surfaces of the same extent can have different frequencies: a permanent
reservoir and a seasonal floodplain occupy the same area and differ in that
proportion.

\subsection{Flood envelope}

\begin{ficha}
  \item[Panel] \texttt{Flood envelope}
  \item[Preconditions] An area, and at least two elevation products selected
  \item[Output] The envelope by threshold, an agreement raster, and a per-run
        assumptions block
\end{ficha}

Flood extent is derived from the terrain, from the height of each cell above
the drainage it flows to. Disagreement between elevation products is reported
as the uncertainty rather than aggregated into a mean.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/flood-envelope.png}
  {\texttt{Flood envelope}. The reading is presented in collapsible blocks:
   agreement over the area, the products used, the envelope by threshold, and
   the assumptions.}
  {fig:flood}

\begin{objecao}
A threshold in metres orders relative susceptibility. It does not correspond to
water depth, extent or probability: rainfall, discharge, propagation and
channel geometry do not enter the calculation. The figure shows where the
result depends on the choice of elevation product.

The product set here differs from the one published in the reference study. The
catalogue this application reads carries no SRTMGL1, and ALOS World 3D stands
in its place. The range measured here is over that different set, and the
published range does not apply to it.
\end{objecao}

\section{The Studio}

\begin{ficha}
  \item[How to open] The button on the right of the header (item~6 of
        figure~\ref{fig:anatomy}), or from a run in the \texttt{Project hub}
  \item[Preconditions] At least one saved run. \texttt{Comparison},
        \texttt{Domain shift} and the \texttt{Between two runs} reading need
        two
  \item[Output] None in itself. The readings are read on screen and exported
        through section~7
\end{ficha}

The Studio opens over the map and divides the screen into areas. Each area
holds an \termo{editor}, and any editor can occupy any area: position is a
property of the arrangement, not of the editor. Dragging a division moves both
sides at once, and two areas cannot overlap.

\campo{Why it exists} Two runs of areas hundreds of kilometres apart cannot be
placed side by side on the map, because the map puts them where they are. The
Studio lifts them off their coordinates.

\campo{Procedure: putting two runs on the board}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=.3em]
  \item Open the Studio with one run loaded.
  \item In the \texttt{Outliner} area, below the tree, use the add control. The
        list is grouped by project, with the period on each row, since the
        period is what separates two runs of the same project. There is a
        search by name.
  \item When there is nothing to add, the list says which of three cases
        applies: \msg{No run matches that.}, \msg{No saved runs yet.} or
        \msg{Every saved run is already on the board.}
  \item Switch to the \texttt{Compare} or \texttt{Diagnose} arrangement.
\end{enumerate}

\campo{Procedure: choosing which pair is compared} The \texttt{Comparison}
editor reads two slots, named in its header. Empty, each slot follows the
selection and says so (\texttt{Follow selection}). Pinned, it holds the chosen
plane while the selection moves on. With three or more planes selected, pinning
is what determines which pair is being read; without it the answer changes
under the reader.

\subsection{\texttt{Compare}: two runs}

For the selected pair the editor gives the scene count of each run, the mean
confidence, the class count, the two predicted planes identified by the model
that produced them, the two confidence maps, the class transition matrix and
the difference list. Where a run carries no such plane, the corresponding space
states \msg{No prediction} or \msg{No confidence map} rather than sitting empty.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/studio-compare.png}
  {\texttt{Compare}. The comparison takes the lower half at full width; the two
   runs stay visible above it.}
  {fig:studio-compare}

\subsection{\texttt{Diagnose}: distance between domains}

It answers one question: does a model fitted in one region still apply in
another? The metrics in table~\ref{tab:domain} measure the distance between the
two. The last four require MapBiomas reference cover over the area.

\terrafig{png-images/impressao/studio-diagnose-domain-shift.png}
  {\texttt{Diagnose}. On the right, the metrics of table~\ref{tab:domain} and
   the histograms of the two areas overlaid. Lower left,
   \texttt{Class separability}.}
  {fig:studio-diagnose}

\subsection{\texttt{Class separability}: where separation is lost}

It measures the distance between two classes band by band, by the
Jeffries-Matusita distance. The scale saturates at 2, and the bands in
table~\ref{tab:jm} are drawn behind the bars: they are a reading convention,
not a result. The shift between classes is reported in percentage points of
reflectance, with a sign.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering
\caption{Reading bands for the Jeffries-Matusita distance.}
\label{tab:jm}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{3.0cm}L{4.0cm}L{8.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Band} & \textbf{Label} & \textbf{Reading} \\
\midrule
0 to 1.0 & \texttt{overlapping} & the two class distributions overlap in that band \\
1.0 to 1.9 & \texttt{partial} & partial separation \\
1.9 to 2.0 & \texttt{separable} & separated; 2 is the ceiling of the scale \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

There are two readings, and they can diverge. \texttt{Against the surround}
measures each pair of classes within one run: how far a class stands apart from
those around it. \texttt{Between two runs} measures the same class in both
runs: whether its response still resembles the one in the other area. A class
can hold a stable spectral response across the two runs and still become
inseparable, if the contrast with neighbouring classes drops.

\begin{objecao}
The distances are \termo{univariate}: one band at a time. A classifier reads the
bands together and separates classes that no single band separates. A column of
low bars, such as the one in figure~\ref{fig:studio-diagnose}, does not indicate
that those classes are indistinguishable; it indicates that no single band
distinguishes them in that case. The panel records this caveat on the screen
itself. The corrections tested against real data across regions did not remove
the difference.
\end{objecao}

\subsection{\texttt{Rover} and \texttt{Spectral response}}

The \texttt{Rover} points at a predicted pixel and reports its class, its
position and what it reflects. Beside the \texttt{Spectral response}, sweeping
the raster lights up the curve of the class under the pointer, band by band.

\begin{objecao}
The curve corresponds to \termo{one acquisition}, not to the seasonal mean. Two
dates of the same class produce different curves, and comparing them holds only
within the acquisition on screen.
\end{objecao}
